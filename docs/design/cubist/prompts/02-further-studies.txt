Use case: stylized-concept.
Create a new, beautifully designed concept sheet of SIX original Cubist mahjong tile faces for a collection named "Cubist". This is the second study for a game whose existing Matisse set uses large, legible, full-bleed portrait 3:4 artwork.

Presentation: near-square contact sheet, 3 columns and 2 rows, six large flat upright 3:4 faces. Warm off-white presentation page. Refined small black heading "CUBIST", subtitle "02 / FURTHER STUDIES". Each face has its caption outside and below it. Even spacing, generous room for the artwork. Flat artwork, no perspective mockups, no tile bevels, no shadows, no inset ivory rim. Colour reaches all four edges of every face.

Visual language: sophisticated synthetic Cubism, overlapping angular painted planes, unexpected but coherent shifts in perspective, strong broken charcoal contours, broad recognisable shapes, offset surfaces, matte pigment and subtle paper texture. A sense of hand-painted collage and early Cubist composition. Ochre, parchment, dusty cobalt, oxblood, vermilion and forest green. Composition varies from tile to tile while maintaining the collection identity. Avoid generic low-poly rendering, excessive small triangles, kaleidoscopes, stained glass, photorealistic gemstones, gratuitous confetti and organic Matisse cut-outs. Keep the principal symbols very large and legible.

Tiles in exact reading order:
A, caption "A — THREE DISKS": EXACTLY THREE separate large circular disks, one high on the left, one in the middle on the right, one low on the left. Each disk has one continuous obvious outside circular contour and an interior formed from a few offset ochre, rust-red and blue planes with one dark off-centre aperture. Cream and blue background planes. Large open gaps, no extra circles.
B, caption "B — FIVE BAMBOO": EXACTLY FIVE complete distinct jointed bamboo stalks, arranged in a quincunx: four green stalks at the corners and one vermilion stalk in the centre. Each has two broad angular segments and one small attached leaf; broad gaps distinguish all five. Green, mint and emerald against deep green with the single red central stalk. No extra thin vertical background marks that could be counted as stalks. Keep all five complete silhouettes within the face.
C, caption "C — NINE CHARACTERS": Correct readable Chinese 九 above 萬, made of broad angular brushlike planes: large ochre 九 in the upper half, ivory and red 萬 in the lower half, with a few intersecting cobalt planes on charcoal. Preserve the proper structure of both glyphs. No Arabic number, no mirrored glyphs, no extra text. Strong asymmetry and clean open counters.
D, caption "D — EAST WIND": One oversized correct readable Chinese 東, reinterpreted as a forceful cubist painted sign. Its central rectangular enclosure has a clear internal horizontal crossbar, a central vertical passes through and extends above and below, and the lower left/right strokes splay outward. Ochre and charcoal glyph planes on parchment with rusty-red and cobalt accents in the background. Do not substitute a different character or human face.
E, caption "E — WHITE DRAGON": A broad angular cobalt-blue frame enclosing a large quiet ivory empty centre. The frame bends through subtly displaced cubist planes of dusty blue, ultramarine and a little charcoal; its outer blue field reaches every edge of the portrait face. Deliberately asymmetric inner contour, sculptural and restrained. The ivory centre remains blank and open: no writing, no animal, no extra decorative symbols. This is a white dragon mahjong symbol rendered through negative space.
F, caption "F — GREEN DRAGON": One very large correct readable Chinese 發, built from interlocking angular green-painted strokes on deep forest green. Pale mint and sage facets make the recognisable glyph strong and legible. Only shades of green anywhere inside this face, including all background planes and outlines: forest, bottle green, emerald, sage and pale mint. No black, ivory, yellow, blue or red. No dragon animal. Keep the proper glyph structure.

The captions and sheet title must be clearly typeset and spelled exactly as above. No captions, Latin suit labels or numbers inside the tile art. Prioritise exact counts and recognisable Chinese glyphs while creating expressive, collectible miniature Cubist paintings.
